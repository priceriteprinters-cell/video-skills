# Asset Design

Derive the asset list from the actual narration or brief, not a fixed count. A process scene might require an isolated device, input object, output object and background; a product explanation may need supplied genuine imagery plus neutral surrounding artwork. Give the animator separable useful layers rather than baked-in controls, captions or all-in-one collages.

Carry ratio/theme/semantic palette from the video brief. Provide backgrounds at useful canvas resolution and isolated items with enough pixel detail for planned zooms. Avoid cutting off shadows or limbs on cutouts. Leave appropriate negative space for live titles and captions. Keep consistent perspective, subject scale, contrast, highlights, shadow direction, texture and outlines. Use transparent PNG only when compositing needs it; document opaque backgrounds.

Make prompts specific to each scene purpose while repeating the approved visual-language constraints. Refer to existing artwork for continuity through current supported edit/reference tools. Do not invent fonts, prices, UI claims or product details. Do not turn prohibited/missing official logos into fake marks. Use explicit neutral explanatory illustration instead and disclose material departures.

Review each delivered PNG, not a low-resolution preview: decoding, resolution, alpha, edge halos, wrong object identity, accidental words, distortions, inconsistent perspective, repeated assets and room for typography. The validator checks bytes/dimensions/provenance records, not artistic quality. Record reviewed=true only after actual visual inspection. If a generation or edit fails terminally, stop that blocked request, preserve successful work, and disclose the partial result; do not evade policy by changing tools/models/prompts.
